### Added

- `router.ws` and `Response.webSocket` take `assembleTimeout` (`Response.defaultWebSocketAssembleTimeout`, 30 s: how long a message, in one frame or in fragments, may take to arrive once it began), `controlFrameBurst` (`Response.defaultWebSocketControlFrameBurst`, 20) and `controlFramesPerSecond` (`Response.defaultWebSocketControlFramesPerSecond`, 5): how many PING and PONG frames a client may send, as a burst and then per second, before the connection is closed with `1008`. `Response.webSocketAssembleTimeout`, `Response.webSocketControlFrameBurst` and `Response.webSocketControlFramesPerSecond` read them.

### Fixed

- Security: WebSocket connections are bounded in what a client can make the server hold. `maxPayloadBytes` limited each frame, not each message, so a client could send a message in fragments of any number and size and the worker buffered all of them; a fragmented message above the limit is now closed with `1009` as it grows, before it reaches the handler, and one that does not complete within `assembleTimeout` with `1008`. PING frames were answered without limit and their PONGs queued without bound when the client did not read, so one connection could grow the worker's memory by tens of MiB a second; PINGs and PONGs are now limited per connection (`1008` above the limit) and the answers count toward `maxBufferedBytes` like any other frame, which closes a client that does not read them as a slow client. A PING between two fragments of a message no longer corrupts the message. Frames are read by the server before `dart:io` sees them; unfragmented messages pass unchanged, and permessage-deflate, `pingInterval` and the slow-client close codes behave as before. Fragmented messages since 1.14.0 (before it, no limit applied at all); PINGs since 1.6.0. Until you upgrade, limit WebSocket connections and handshakes per client IP at the proxy (nginx `limit_conn` and `limit_req` on the upgrade location): a proxy does not limit frames once a connection is upgraded.
- A WebSocket session ended only when the server stopped if its handler neither wrote to the socket nor closed it, even after the client closed or dropped the connection: `dart:io` completes a server socket's `done` only once its sink is closed. Each such connection kept its scope and its slot in the server's count of open WebSockets. The socket is now closed from the server side when the client goes away. Since 1.6.0.

### Upgrade notes

- The Pusher server closes a message above `REVERB_APP_MAX_MESSAGE_SIZE` with `1009`, as Reverb does; it allowed 1 KiB more before.
- Reading the frames before `dart:io` costs a few percent on incoming WebSocket messages (2 to 10 % for 1 KB messages, 4 to 6 % for 64 KB, in a local benchmark); sending is not affected.
- A client that sends PING frames faster than 20 at once and then 5 a second is now closed with `1008`. Browsers never send PINGs; raise `controlFrameBurst` and `controlFramesPerSecond` on routes whose clients ping often.
- New public names in `package:daravel/daravel.dart`: `Response.defaultWebSocketAssembleTimeout`, `Response.defaultWebSocketControlFrameBurst`, `Response.defaultWebSocketControlFramesPerSecond`, `Response.webSocketAssembleTimeout`, `Response.webSocketControlFrameBurst`, `Response.webSocketControlFramesPerSecond`, and the `assembleTimeout`, `controlFrameBurst` and `controlFramesPerSecond` parameters of `Response.webSocket` and `router.ws`.
