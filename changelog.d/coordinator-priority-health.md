### Added

- Coordinator priorities and health (Blueprint #141, K1; #148). A coordinator service says how urgent its calls are with `PrioritizedService` (`priority`, and `priorityFor(op, args)` per operation): `CoordinatorPriority.critical` (rate limits, the JWT denylist, the `process` cache's locks) is always served at once; `normal` (the `process` cache, presence channels, and any service that does not say) is put off while the coordinator is saturated and still answered; `bulk` (broadcast fan-out, metrics, worker reports, Horizon's job records) is put off while it is lagging and refused with `CoordinatorOverloaded` while it is saturated. The coordinator is `lagging` after three lag measurements at `COORDINATOR_LAG_WARN_MS` (5) and `saturated` at `COORDINATOR_LAG_SHED_MS` (50) or with `maxQueue` calls put off; it gets better only after `Coordinator.recoverySamples` (3) quiet measurements in a row. Workers hear the state (`CoordinatorHealth`, deeply immutable, sent on each change, each second while unhealthy, and to a worker first heard from while unhealthy before its reply): `RemoteCoordinatorChannel.coordinatorState`, `onStateChange`, and shed services failed in the worker without a round trip (`onShortCircuit`); the rate limiter then applies the limit's failure policy at once instead of after its timeout. `/metrics`: `coordinator_state{state}` and `coordinator_short_circuit_total{service}` per worker, `coordinator_health{state}`, `coordinator_deferred`, `coordinator_deferred_peak`, `coordinator_shed_total{service}` and `coordinator_served_total{priority}` for the coordinator. `Coordinator(lagWarn:, lagShed:, probeEvery:)` and `observeLag` (for tests).
- Broadcasts through this server are best effort (K1): when the coordinator cannot take one (`CoordinatorOverloaded`, `CoordinatorTimeoutException`), `Broadcast.send` and `ShouldBroadcast` events no longer fail the request that sent them, after a commit included: the broadcast is dropped, logged (a warning at most once a second, with the count), counted in `/metrics` `broadcast_shed_total{reason}` (`overloaded`, `timeout`) and dispatched as the application event `BroadcastShed` (event, channels, reason). Inside a job a worker runs the error is raised instead, so the job is retried (a `sync` job, run inline and never retried, drops and tells as a request does); any other error is raised as before; the `reverb`, `pusher`, `log` and `null` connections are unchanged. Guide: [Broadcasting](docs/guide/broadcasting.md#where-events-go).

### Changed

- The coordinator's lag probe (`queue_lag_ms`) measures how long a message to its own isolate waits behind the others, or how late its timer fired past `Coordinator.defaultTimerSlack` (16 ms), whichever is larger. It used to measure only the timer, which on Windows fires 5 to 8 ms late on an idle loop (timer resolution 15.6 ms), so an idle coordinator there read several milliseconds of lag.
- A broadcast the coordinator cannot take, possible under a flood since the priorities above, does not become a 500 (nor a 500 after the transaction committed): it is dropped and told, as described under Added.

### Upgrade notes

- Coordinator (K1): new public names `CoordinatorPriority`, `PrioritizedService`, `CoordinatorState`, `CoordinatorHealth`, `CoordinatorOverloaded` and `BroadcastShed` in `package:daravel` (an application class of one of these names and the barrel import give an ambiguous-import error; `hide` it or rename yours). A coordinator service of a package or an application that does not mix in `PrioritizedService` is `normal`: while the coordinator is saturated its calls wait (they are never refused); mark telemetry that may be lost as `bulk`, and locks or limits as `critical`. While saturated, `broadcast`, `metrics` and `workerReport` calls fail with `CoordinatorOverloaded` (counted in `coordinator_shed_total`) instead of queueing behind the critical ones.
