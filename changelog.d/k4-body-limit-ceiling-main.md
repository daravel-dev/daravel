### Fixed

- `BodyLimitsMiddleware` no longer raises a lower `HTTP_MAX_BODY` (`ServeOptions.maxBodyBytes`) to its own content-type limit: with `HTTP_MAX_BODY` at 64 KiB a 150 000 byte JSON body was served, because the middleware replaced the limit the request arrived with by its 1 MiB for JSON and forms and 50 MiB for other bodies. Its limits still replace the engine's `HTTP_MAX_JSON_BODY` and `HTTP_MAX_FORM_BODY` (also with a larger value), but the smaller of its limit and `HTTP_MAX_BODY` now applies.
- A route's `bodyLimit` above the limit of a global `BodyLimitsMiddleware` is honoured for a body with a `Content-Length`: the middleware answered `413` before the route was known, so only a body without a declared length (chunked) reached the route's limit. It now leaves that answer to the kernel, which gives it once the route is looked up, before the route's middleware and handler.
- `dartisan doctor` shows the limits of `BodyLimitsMiddleware` as they apply, never above `HTTP_MAX_BODY`.

### Upgrade notes

- Upgrading can change which bodies an application accepts: a limit of `BodyLimitsMiddleware` (`json`, `form`, `other`) above `HTTP_MAX_BODY` no longer applies, so a body between the two is now refused with `413`; raise `HTTP_MAX_BODY`, or give the route a `bodyLimit`, which replaces every other limit and may be larger than `HTTP_MAX_BODY`. A route whose `bodyLimit` is above the middleware's limit now accepts a declared body up to its `bodyLimit`, which was refused before.
- With `BodyLimitsMiddleware` as global middleware, the `413` for a declared `Content-Length` over the limit now comes after the route lookup instead of from the middleware itself: the global middleware after it runs first (the route's middleware and handler still do not), and a request no route matches is answered `413` rather than `404`, `405` or by `withUpstream`. A form `POST` that `MethodOverrideMiddleware` reads first gets the same `413` through the exception handler, so `report` and `render` callbacks for `PayloadTooLargeHttpException` see it. On a route or a route group the middleware answers at once, as before.
