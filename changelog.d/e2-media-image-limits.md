### Fixed

- Security: `daravel_medialibrary` decoded an image for its conversions whatever size it declared: a 995 368-byte PNG of 16 000 x 16 000 RGBA pixels took 2.2 GiB of memory (an isolate shares its process's memory), and other formats could do the same. Images are now decoded only as JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP or BMP, told from the content, each by a decoder held to `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (40 million pixels, width x height x frames; a 16-bit PNG and a WebP count twice) and `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` (1 000), with the size read from the header before anything is decoded; a still image is decoded as its one frame, and a JPEG must have 1 or 3 color components. Past a limit, `ImageTooLargeException`: the conversion is not made, the job fails at once instead of three times, and the original stays stored (the same PNG now peaks at 16 MiB). The EXIF orientation is baked only when there is one to apply. Since 1.0.0.
- Security: TIFF is not converted in this version: a TIFF keeps its original and gets no conversions, and `canConvert` answers `false` for it. Animated PNG (APNG) and animated WebP are not converted in this version either: such a file keeps its original and gets no conversions (`ImageConversionException`); a still PNG or WebP and an animated GIF are converted. ICO, TGA, PVR, EXR, PNM and PSD content is not converted either, whatever the file is named.
- Security: image conversions bound the work of decoding and manipulating an image. Every image made by a resize, crop or canvas holds at most a quarter of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (frames counted), blur is bounded by its pixels, frames and radius, an image read or made has sides of at most 65 535, and a progressive JPEG with more scans than its size allows is not converted. Invalid manipulation dimensions are rejected consistently and image encoder errors use `ImageConversionException`; past a work limit, `ImageTooLargeException`. Since 1.0.0.
- `Fit.fill` and `Fit.fillMax` on an animated GIF keep every frame, each with its duration, on the white canvas; they kept only the first.
- The JPEG scan count before decoding skips what the segments before the first scan carry (an EXIF thumbnail, a comment), as a decoder does, so bytes there are no longer counted as scans.
- A conversion writes a GIF of at most `MEDIA_MAX_GIF_PIXELS` pixels (width x height x frames; a tenth of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS`, 4 million, by default), refused before encoding past it: encoding a GIF costs about 1.1 to 1.2 s per million pixels of a photograph. A 16-bit image is written as a GIF through 8 bits, which the GIF encoder could not take.
- Blur counts its work by depth and channels: a 16-bit image eleven times an 8-bit one, an RGBA image 4/3 and a grayscale one 1/3 of three channels; a 1, 2 or 4-bit image is blurred as 8 bits. Blur is not applied to other pixel formats.
- An `Error` the image library throws reading, decoding or encoding a file becomes an `ImageLibraryException` (an `ImageConversionException`) with the error's type, logged as a warning: the conversion job fails once instead of decoding the same file three times. Running out of memory or stack is thrown as it is.

### Upgrade notes

- Upgrading can leave conversions ungenerated: for TIFF files and animated PNG or WebP files (until a later version; the original stays stored), for an image larger than 40 million pixels (width x height x frames) or with more than 1 000 frames (raise `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` / `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` if such images are expected), and for image content other than JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP and BMP whatever the file is named; the failed conversion job holds the reason. `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` or `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` below 1 stops the boot. `ImageConversionException` is now a `base` class. New public names: `ImageTooLargeException`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImageFrames`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImageFrames`, and the `maxPixels`/`maxFrames` parameters of `convertImage`/`convertImageSync`.
- Upgrading can leave conversions ungenerated for an image with a side longer than 65 535 pixels, for any resize, crop or canvas output past a quarter of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (10 million pixels at the default; 1080p to 4K still converts), for still-image blur past 80 million pixel-radius units or animated blur past 20 million pixel-frame-radius units, for a non-positive manipulation dimension, or for a JPEG with more scans than its size allows; the original file stays stored. Put `width` or `height` before `blur` when a conversion needs both. At the still-image boundary, 5 MP measured 2.8 s (3.9 s on a slower reference machine) and 10 MP measured 3.5 to 3.6 s (4.8 to 5.0 s on that machine); a 1 MP image can reach only 50 million units at blur's maximum radius and measured 1.4 to 1.8 s (2.0 to 2.4 s). Animated blur at 20 million units measured 3.2 to 4.4 s. A 10-million-pixel resize measured 4.5 to 7.2 s depending on shape and machine; encoding an unchanged 40-million-pixel image measured 3.0 to 6.7 s.
- Upgrading can leave a photo above 40 million pixels (a phone's 48 or 50 MP mode, for example) without conversions unless `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` is raised; the original file stays stored.
- Upgrading can leave a WebP above 20 million pixels without conversions at the default limit, since a WebP counts against half of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS`; raise it to twice the largest WebP expected. The original file stays stored.
- Converting an image at the default limit still takes memory: about 130 MiB for a baseline JPEG of 8 000 x 5 000, about 415 MiB for a progressive one, about 205 MiB for a PNG of 6 324 x 6 324, up to about 470 MiB for a WebP, and about 140 MiB for an animated GIF filling the budget (measured peaks); plan about 500 MiB per conversion running at once, whatever the size of the image, since the limit bounds an image's size, not memory exactly; lower `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` where conversions share a process with requests.
- Upgrading can leave a GIF conversion ungenerated past `MEDIA_MAX_GIF_PIXELS` (4 million pixels by default, for example an animation of 500 x 500 in 60 frames, which it used to encode slowly): make it smaller first, choose another format, or raise `MEDIA_MAX_GIF_PIXELS` (about 1.2 s per million pixels).
- Upgrading can leave a blurred conversion of a 16-bit image, or of an RGBA one near the limit, ungenerated well below the size an 8-bit RGB image allows: resize before blur. A whole conversion at the default limits can take up to about 19 s (26 s on a slower machine): run conversions on the queue with a job timeout above that.
