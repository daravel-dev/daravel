### Fixed

- Security: `daravel_medialibrary` decoded an image for its conversions whatever size it declared: a 995 368-byte PNG of 16 000 x 16 000 RGBA pixels took 2.2 GiB of memory (an isolate shares its process's memory), and other formats could do the same. Images are now decoded only as JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP or BMP, told from the content, each by a decoder held to `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (40 million pixels, width x height x frames; a 16-bit PNG and a WebP count twice) and `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` (1 000), with the size read from the header before anything is decoded; a still image is decoded as its one frame, and a JPEG must have 1 or 3 color components. Past a limit, `ImageTooLargeException`: the conversion is not made, the job fails at once instead of three times, and the original stays stored (the same PNG now peaks at 16 MiB). The EXIF orientation is baked only when there is one to apply. Since 1.0.0.
- Security: TIFF is not converted in this version: a TIFF keeps its original and gets no conversions, and `canConvert` answers `false` for it. Animated PNG (APNG) and animated WebP are not converted in this version either: such a file keeps its original and gets no conversions (`ImageConversionException`); a still PNG or WebP and an animated GIF are converted. ICO, TGA, PVR, EXR, PNM and PSD content is not converted either, whatever the file is named.
- Security: `daravel_medialibrary` bounded the image it read but not the images its manipulations made: `width()`, `height()` and `Fit.crop` follow the aspect ratio, so a small, very thin file could grow into hundreds of millions of pixels and minutes of work, and a very long side cost time even with few pixels. A manipulation now makes at most the larger of the image's own pixels and a quarter of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (frames counted), an image read or made has sides of at most 65 535 (the most a JPEG or GIF holds), and a progressive JPEG with more scans than its size allows is not converted (each scan is a pass over the image); past a limit, `ImageTooLargeException` before the image is made. Since 1.0.0.

### Upgrade notes

- Upgrading can leave conversions ungenerated: for TIFF files and animated PNG or WebP files (until a later version; the original stays stored), for an image larger than 40 million pixels (width x height x frames) or with more than 1 000 frames (raise `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` / `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` if such images are expected), and for image content other than JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP and BMP whatever the file is named; the failed conversion job holds the reason. `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` or `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` below 1 stops the boot. `ImageConversionException` is now a `base` class. New public names: `ImageTooLargeException`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImageFrames`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImageFrames`, and the `maxPixels`/`maxFrames` parameters of `convertImage`/`convertImageSync`.
- Upgrading can leave conversions ungenerated for an image with a side longer than 65 535 pixels, for a conversion that would enlarge an image past a quarter of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (10 million pixels at the default; 1080p to 4K still converts), or of a very elongated image to a width or height that its ratio makes huge, and for a JPEG with more scans than its size allows; the original file stays stored. A manipulation's cost follows the pixels it makes: about 3.3 s for 10 million.
- Upgrading can leave a photo above 40 million pixels (a phone's 48 or 50 MP mode, for example) without conversions unless `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` is raised; the original file stays stored.
- Upgrading can leave a WebP above 20 million pixels without conversions at the default limit, since a WebP counts against half of `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS`; raise it to twice the largest WebP expected. The original file stays stored.
- Converting an image at the default limit still takes memory: about 130 MiB for a baseline JPEG of 8 000 x 5 000, about 415 MiB for a progressive one, about 205 MiB for a PNG of 6 324 x 6 324, up to about 470 MiB for a WebP, and about 140 MiB for an animated GIF filling the budget (measured peaks); plan about 500 MiB per conversion running at once, whatever the size of the image, since the limit bounds an image's size, not memory exactly; lower `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` where conversions share a process with requests.
