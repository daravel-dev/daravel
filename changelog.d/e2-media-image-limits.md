### Fixed

- Security: `daravel_medialibrary` decoded an image for its conversions whatever size it declared: a 995 368-byte PNG of 16 000 x 16 000 RGBA pixels took 2.2 GiB of memory (an isolate shares its process's memory), and other formats could do the same. Images are now decoded only as JPEG, PNG (APNG too), GIF, WebP or BMP, told from the content, each by a decoder held to `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` (40 million pixels, width x height x frames; a 16-bit PNG counts twice) and `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` (1 000), with the size read from the header before anything is decoded; an animated WebP frame whose bitstream size differs from its frame header is refused. Past a limit, `ImageTooLargeException`: the conversion is not made, the job fails at once instead of three times, and the original stays stored (the same PNG now peaks at 16 MiB). The EXIF orientation is baked only when there is one to apply. Since 1.0.0.
- Security: TIFF is not converted in this version: a TIFF keeps its original and gets no conversions, and `canConvert` answers `false` for it. ICO, TGA, PVR, EXR, PNM and PSD content is not converted either, whatever the file is named.

### Upgrade notes

- Upgrading can leave conversions ungenerated: for TIFF files (until a later version), for an image larger than 40 million pixels (width x height x frames) or with more than 1 000 frames (raise `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` / `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` if such images are expected), and for image content other than JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP and BMP whatever the file is named; the failed conversion job holds the reason. `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` or `MEDIA_MAX_IMAGE_FRAMES` below 1 stops the boot. `ImageConversionException` is now a `base` class. New public names: `ImageTooLargeException`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.maxImageFrames`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImagePixels`, `MediaLibraryConfig.defaultMaxImageFrames`, and the `maxPixels`/`maxFrames` parameters of `convertImage`/`convertImageSync`.
- Known limitation: a small PNG whose `IDAT` inflates to far more than its size needs (a 100 x 100 image with 64 MiB of zeros) still takes about 145 MiB to convert; bounded.
